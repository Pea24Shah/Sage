Honey bees live in colonies that usually contain one queen, several hundred drones, and between 20,000 and 60,000 workers during summer. The queen can lay up to 2,000 eggs per day in the busiest season. Worker bees live about six weeks in summer but several months in winter. Foragers communicate the direction and distance of food with a movement called the waggle dance. A hive keeps its brood nest at roughly 35 degrees Celsius by clustering together and by fanning their wings. Honey bees do not hibernate in winter; instead they form a tight cluster and shiver to keep the queen warm. Colonies need about 20 kilograms of honey to survive a cold winter.
